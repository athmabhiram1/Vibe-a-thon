# 06 — Geek Room Pears Global Hackathon (Mar 2025) — Winners

**Event:** Pears Global Hackathon 2025 (aka Pears Hackathon / PEARS Global Hackathon)
**Organizers:** Geek Room (+ Geek Suite outreach) × Pears by Holepunch (collab with JetBrains for judging)
**Format:** Online global, free, open to all (students/professionals)
**Platform:** DoraHacks (`dorahacks.io/hackathon/geekroom-pears`) + Keet app group for coordination
**Dates:** Submissions due ~25 Feb (extended to 28 Feb) → Round 1 / Mentoring 2 Mar 2025 → Final judging 9-10 Mar → Winners announced 12 Mar (LinkedIn) + 17 Mar 2025 (Holepunch press release)
**Prize pool:** Listed as $5,000 on Geek Room/Luma + tablets/AirPods/headphones; DoraHacks page shows $10,000; organizer LinkedIn posts claim $10K+ / 2K+ participants. Scale reported: 1,000+ registrations, 400+ participants, 90-100+ projects/teams, 12+ countries, Top 8 finalists.
**Mandatory requirement:** All projects must integrate Pears Technology — Pear Runtime / Holepunch stack (Hyperswarm, Hypercore/Hyperdrive/Hyperbee, HyperDHT, Bare).

## How P2P Integration Was Judged

DoraHacks official criteria (`dorahacks.io/hackathon/geekroom-pears/detail`):
1. **Creativity and Innovation** — uniqueness/novelty
2. **Integration** — effective use of Pears technology in the project
3. **Functionality** — usability and robustness
4. **Impact** — real-world application + scalability
5. **Team Collaboration** — quality of teamwork during hackathon

Holepunch press summary (collab Geek Room + JetBrains): “evaluated across creativity, usability and use of P2P technology.”

Judges/mentors named:
- **David Mark Clements** — Pear Platform Principal Architect (Holepunch) — praised fast onboarding to Pear infra; quote: WhereFam exemplifies family safety/security.
- **Guy Swann** — decentralization advocate / Bitcoin Audible host — sovereignty-without-UX-loss thesis; personally picked WhereFam (“I will spend $5 and buy this app right now” — Bitcoin Audible Chat159 podcast).
- **Mathias Buus Madsen** — Holepunch CEO (press quotes, zero-infra-cost mission).
- Mentors: Aryan Gupta, Priya Chandak (Round 1 per Kavya Raj post).

What “good P2P integration” meant in practice:
- **Eliminate server for a naturally P2P activity** (chess face-to-face → direct connection; whiteboard collaboration; location share with chosen peers only).
- **Use Pear Runtime + Hyperswarm/Hypercore/HyperDHT/Bare** for discovery (topics/room IDs), hole-punched encrypted connections, live state sync — not a transport wrapper. Winners cite: Hyperswarm networking, hypercore-crypto + b4a (Chess); Hyperswarm-powered low-latency drawing (Hypersketch); Bare Runtime embedding in Swift/Kotlin + Hypercore/Hyperswarm/DHT + UDP hole punching + hypercore-blobs tile server for offline maps (WhereFam — deepest stack).
- **Privacy/sovereignty narrative + zero-infra cost** — no tracking, no cloud, every device client+server. WhereFam won on pure privacy story, not tech depth (per competitor analysis).
- **UX parity with centralized incumbents** — Swann: “sovereignty back, without losing functionality.” Polish + runnable `pear run` demo mattered.

## Patterns for Future Pears Builds

- Crowded (avoid clones): chat/messaging, file-share/drive/clipboard, real-time games, collab whiteboard/docs, location sharing.
- Underserved (white space noted by later Tether Cup research citing this hackathon): payments/value-transfer/settlement/marketplace/escrow — essentially absent; Autobase for financial data = differentiator.
- Winning formula: single-purpose app + “no servers to hack” + real Pear depth (swarm topics, direct sync, local persistence) + live demo + family/safety or creator-collab framing.
- Tech keywords judges respond to: Hyperswarm topics, HyperDHT keys (not IPs), Noise-encrypted connections, Hypercore append-only log, Corestore/Autobase for multiwriter, Bare embed, `pear run` runnable.
