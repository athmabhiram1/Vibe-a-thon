## Patterns (what won / what the room built)

1. **Voice + memory was the house formula.** Every team had to ship Vapi (voice) + Qdrant (retrieval/memory); winners differentiated on the orchestration around that core (dating-agent matching, local-only inference, SRE runbook retrieval).
2. **Overall winner was consumer/social, not enterprise.** Konichiwa (AI-agent dating) beat hardcore infra builds — demoable chemistry-matching narrative + "5 stood on stage" pitch mattered more than technical depth.
3. **Local/private AI was a Top-5 theme.** Hey Dot's pitch — "no cloud, no internet, nothing leaves your machine, sub-second" (Ollama + Moondream) — shows privacy/local inference played well with judges.
4. **DevTools-for-DevOps scored Honorable Mention.** Token Titans' Autonomous SRE (detect → retrieve runbook → voice-call the human → execute fix via MCP→Docker) is the most architecturally detailed build found; judges validated it but it finished outside Top 5 — stings as "most technical ≠ winner."
5. **Healthcare voice assistants clustered.** VoiceRx (solo, Top 40) and 2care.ai (finalist) both did Vapi+Qdrant+RAG health Q&A — the most crowded track.
6. **Solo builders could final.** At least two solo/small entries (Karan's Team; Harsh Maurya) reached finals; team size 1–4 was allowed.
7. **Hiring/dev-rel undercurrent.** 100+ companies in room, "professionals outnumbered students" framing, internship/hiring perks listed — this was a recruiting floor as much as a hackathon (matters for outreach angle: participants are employed devs, not just students).
